2.  **The Launch Threshold:** The satellite population $S$ is driven by the launch rate $L$ and limited by the operational life $T$. In the absence of collisions, $S$ would stabilize at $L \cdot T$.
    *   If $L \cdot T < 400$, then $S$ will settle below the threshold, causing debris to decay because $f \cdot c < N/\tau$. For example, at $L=40$, $S$ approaches $40 \times 5 = 200$, which is well below 400.
    *   If $L \cdot T > 400$, the satellite population is high enough to produce more debris than the atmosphere can remove, leading to an explosion in the debris population. For example, at $L=120$, $S$ approaches $120 \times 5 = 600$, which is well above 400.
    *   The critical launch rate is therefore $L = 400 / 5 = \mathbf{80 \text{ launches/year}}$.

**Limits:** These conclusions are specific to this toy model. In reality, the relationship between $L, S,$ and $N$ is much more complex due to non-linearities in orbital mechanics and varying atmospheric densities.
