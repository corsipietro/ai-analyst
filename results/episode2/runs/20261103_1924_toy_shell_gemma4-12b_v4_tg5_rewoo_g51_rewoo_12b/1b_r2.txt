The simulation reveals a clear correlation between the launch rate and the final population of both satellites and debris:
*   **Satellites:** At a low launch rate of 40, the satellite population decreases over time, reaching a mean of **385** at year 60 [tool]. At the highest launch rate of 120, the population grows significantly, reaching a mean of **1,709** at year 60 [tool].
*   **Debris:** In all tested scenarios, the amount of debris increases over time. For a launch rate of 40, the debris population reaches a mean of **570** at year 60 [tool]. For a launch rate of 120, it reaches a mean of **2,827** [tool].

### Conclusions
Increasing the launch rate significantly increases both the number of satellites and the amount of debris:
*   **Satellite Population Difference:** The difference in the final number of satellites between the 40 and 120 launch rates is **1,324** [computed: 1709 - 385].
*   **Debris Population Difference:** The difference in the final amount of debris between the 40 and 120 launch rates is **2,257** [computed: 2827 - 570].
*   **Statistical Confidence:** These differences are statistically significant, as the differences in averages across the entire simulation period were already established with a 95% confidence level [tool].

### Limits
The conclusions are based on 3 repetitions per configuration [tool]. Because the number of samples is small, the standard deviations (spread) are relatively high (e.g., a spread of 2,872 for debris at a 120 launch rate), which means that while the mean trend is clear, individual run outcomes can vary significantly. The model is a simplified representation of orbital mechanics and may not perfectly capture the complex dynamics of real-world orbital debris.
