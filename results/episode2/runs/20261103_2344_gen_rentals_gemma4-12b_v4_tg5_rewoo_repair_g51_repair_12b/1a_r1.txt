### What is not solid
Several factors limit the certainty of these conclusions:
1.  **Measurement Bias (Aggregated Rows)**: The averages for the subscription plans are calculated over individual rental records. Because one customer can have up to 317 rows (repeated rentals), these figures represent the **average behavior of a single rental**, not the average behavior of a unique customer. A "power user" who rides 100 times a month would skew this mean more than a casual user. To fix this, we would need to calculate the mean per `cliente_id` before averaging by plan.
2.  **Missing Data**: 
    *   There are **155 missing values** (approx. 3%) in the `durata_min` (duration) column and **140 missing values** (approx. 2.7%) in `stazione_arrivo`.
    *   **23 customers** (approx. 5.5%) have no recorded age (`eta`), which limits demographic profiling.
3.  **Data Orphans**: There are **11 rentals** with a `cliente_id` that does not exist in the `clienti` table. While a small number, these records cannot be linked to any specific plan or demographic.

**Conclusion**: While the data provides a good overview of volume and immediate costs, the "per-plan" behavior is skewed by usage frequency rather than just plan type. I would recommend a "per-user" analysis to see if certain plans actually encourage more frequent usage before making strategic decisions.
