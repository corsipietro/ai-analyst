### What is Not Solid
Several factors reduce the reliability of these results:
*   **Significant Data Gaps:** The `acquisto_eur` (purchase) column is **93.3% null** [tool] (1,368 nulls out of 1,466 rows). This means the vast majority of sessions did not result in a purchase, and the current purchase data represents a very small subset of the total user base.
*   **High Variability in Web Data:** The **Web** platform has a significantly lower sample size (223 rows) compared to Android (707) and iOS (493). Furthermore, the standard deviation for Web duration is **11.3** [tool], which is quite high, and the trend analysis shows extreme fluctuations (e.g., a low of 3.3 minutes vs. a high of 27.5 minutes) [tool], suggesting the "average" may not represent a typical experience.
*   **Missing Duration Data:** There are **80 null values** [tool] in the `durata_min` column. While this is a small percentage (5.46%), it means the average durations for all platforms are slightly incomplete.
*   **Crash Rate interpretation:** Because the number of crashes is low (e.g., only 1 on Web), it is not statistically "solid" to claim the Web platform is "stable" based on this month alone; a single sample is not enough to establish a trend.

**Limitations:**
These conclusions are limited to the September 2026 data. To make these findings more robust, I would recommend:
1.  Analyzing the "Successful Purchase" rate (converting users who saw certain screens to buyers).
2.  Comparing the crash rate as a *percentage* of total sessions rather than a raw count to see which platform is technically most unstable.
3.  Filtering the Web data to see if specific days of high-variance were caused by outliers or specific events.
