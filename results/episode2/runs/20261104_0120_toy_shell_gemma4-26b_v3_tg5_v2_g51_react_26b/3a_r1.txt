### Mathematical Explanation
The behavior is explained by the balance in the debris evolution equation:
$$\Delta N \approx (f \cdot \alpha \cdot S \cdot N) - \frac{N}{\tau}$$

The debris population grows only if the rate of creation from collisions ($f \cdot \alpha \cdot S \cdot N$) is greater than the rate of removal due to atmospheric drag ($N/\tau$). This occurs when the satellite population $S$ exceeds a critical threshold $S_{threshold}$:
$$S > \frac{1}{f \cdot \alpha \cdot \tau}$$

Using the model parameters ($\alpha = 10^{-6}$, $f = 100$, $\tau = 25$):
$$S_{threshold} = \frac{1}{100 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 25} = \frac{1}{0.0025} = 400 \text{ satellites}$$

*   **When $L$ is low (e.g., $L=40$):** The steady-state satellite population $S$ will be much lower than 400 (approximately $L \times T = 40 \times 5 = 200$). Since $S < 400$, the term $(f \cdot \alpha \cdot S - 1/\tau)$ is negative, and the debris population decays [inference].
*   **When $L$ is high (e.g., $L=120$):** The steady-state population $S$ is much higher than 400 (approximately $120 \times 5 = 600$). Since $S > 400$, the term is positive, leading to the observed exponential-like growth of debris [inference].
