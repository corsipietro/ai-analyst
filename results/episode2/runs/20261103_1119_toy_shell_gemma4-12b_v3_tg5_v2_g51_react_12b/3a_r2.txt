The behavior is governed by the update equation for debris:
$$N(t+1) = \max(0, N + f \cdot c - N/\tau)$$

1.  **Collision Rate ($c$):** The number of collisions depends on the product of active satellites ($S$) and debris ($N$). Because the launch rate $L$ increases the number of satellites $S$, a higher $L$ directly increases the collision rate $c$ over time.
2.  **Production vs. Decay:**
    *   **Removal:** Debris is removed by a term proportional to the current population: $N/\tau$. With $\tau = 25$ years, this represents a steady "decay" of the population.
    *   **Production:** New debris is created by collisions: $f \cdot c$, where $f=100$ is the number of fragments per collision.
3.  **The Threshold:** The system's behavior depends on whether $f \cdot c > N/\tau$. 
    *   At **low $L$**, the growth from collisions is too small to replace the debris removed by the $\tau$ term, leading to a net decrease. 
    *   At **high $L$**, the high number of satellites creates a collision rate $c$ that produces more fragments ($f \cdot c$) than the atmospheric drag can remove ($N/\tau$), leading to the runaway growth observed in the $L=120$ case.
