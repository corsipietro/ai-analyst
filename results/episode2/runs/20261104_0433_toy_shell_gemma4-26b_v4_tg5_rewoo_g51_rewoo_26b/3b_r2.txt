### Explanation via Model Equations
The evolution of debris $N$ is governed by the balance between production from collisions and natural decay:
$$\Delta N \approx (f \cdot c) - \frac{N}{\tau}$$
Substituting the collision equation $c \approx \alpha \cdot S \cdot N$, we get:
$$\Delta N \approx (\alpha \cdot f \cdot S \cdot N) - \frac{N}{\tau}$$

The debris population will grow if the production term is greater than the decay term:
$$\alpha \cdot f \cdot S > \frac{1}{\tau} \implies S > \frac{1}{\alpha \cdot f \cdot \tau}$$

Using the provided parameters ($\alpha = 10^{-6}, f = 100, \tau = 25$):
$$S_{threshold} = \frac{1}{10^{-6} \cdot 100 \cdot 25} = \frac{1}{0.0025} = 400 \text{ satellites}$$

**Conclusion:**
*   If the launch strategy $L$ maintains a satellite population $S$ below **400**, the decay term dominates and debris levels fall [inference].
*   If $L$ maintains $S$ above **400**, the collision-driven production dominates, leading to the explosive growth observed at higher launch rates [inference].
